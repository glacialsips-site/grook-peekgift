Built once, standing ready for 40+ years, fed by captured CO2 the power industry already produces.

01 · THE THREAT

The losses concentrate where value meets recurrent fire.

Eighteen of the twenty costliest US wildfires happened in the last 25 years, and the curve is bending the wrong way. The dollars are not spread across the wilderness — they detonate at a short list of wildland–urban edges that burn again and again.

02 · THE CONCEPT

Defense in depth: three suppression lines behind a treated approach.

The band is not a fence — it is a kill zone for the flame front. A maintained low-fuel approach strip forces any arriving crown fire down to a ground fire; three independent nozzle lines then stop it with flashing liquid CO2 (−109 °F snow + gas) delivered straight into the fuel bed. The ~2-mile standoff behind the band is what defeats ember rain: distance collapses ember arrival ~100×, and interior spokes mop up strays. Hover or tap any zone.

Cross-section · hover a zone
Fire side → 100 ft treated approach strip → three CO2 nozzle lines 100 ft apart (≈500 ft of active hardware) → ~2 mi standoff → community with interior spokes. Band follows the prevailing-wind threat arc, not a full ring.

03 · THE SYSTEM

Spine → hubs → spokes. Aimed by valves, not by people.

Plan view of one defended community. The storage farm and trunk spine sit on the wildland side; sectional hubs feed the three lines and the interior spokes. Everything runs on the CO2's own pressure — no pumps, no grid power — because the grid is down in every one of these events.

Autonomous activation is interlocked to mandatory-evacuation status; manual override always available. Fire-crew exclusion-zone protocol applies during discharge (co-developed with CAL FIRE — open item).

04 · THE SUPPLY

The CO2 is free — the data-center buildout makes it.

New gas-fired data-center campuses are being permitted over community objections everywhere. Condition those permits: capture your stack CO2 and deliver it to the regional fire-defense reserve. The quantities are trivially in the campuses' favor — and delivery is by ordinary cryo truck and rail, which sidesteps California's CO2-pipeline moratorium entirely.

Scale check: the full 20-community Phase-1 initial fill is ≈120 kt — under one month of one campus's captured output. Annual make-up (activations + losses) is 10–30 kt, a rounding error. Interim supply before campuses come online: merchant liquid CO2, ≈$20–40M for the entire portfolio fill.

$5.5–8.5M per defended kilometer, all-in.
