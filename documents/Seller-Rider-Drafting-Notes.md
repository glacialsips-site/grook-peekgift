## B. Legal points to confirm with your attorney (the substantive flags)

**1. The lead‑paint "waiver" — you can waive the inspection, not the disclosure.**
You asked the buyer to "waive the lead paint disclosure." Under federal law (42 U.S.C. § 4852d / the EPA‑HUD rule), the **seller's disclosure** of known lead‑based paint and hazards, delivery of the EPA pamphlet, and the Lead Warning Statement are **mandatory and cannot be waived** — purporting to waive them can expose the seller to penalties (including treble damages). What a buyer *can* waive is the **10‑day opportunity to inspect / risk‑assess**. The base contract already has Section 13 marked "Applicable" with a signed Lead‑Based Paint addendum, so I drafted § 5.4 to waive only the inspection opportunity and to have the buyer acknowledge the disclosure they already received. This gives you the practical effect you want (no lead contingency, sold as‑is) without the unenforceable/risky part. **Recommend leaving it drafted this way.**

**2. Your buyer's mortgage vs. your post‑closing stay (the 60‑day problem).**
The buyers are approved for a $744,000 **conventional** loan. If that loan is for a primary residence, Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac rules generally require the buyer to occupy within ~60 days of closing and effectively cap seller rent‑backs at ~60 days. Your structure (30 days free **plus** an open‑ended escalating rent‑back) could push total occupancy past 60 days and jeopardize the buyer's loan or put them in an occupancy‑affidavit bind. Because we've also made the deal **non‑contingent on financing** (§ 6), that risk falls on the buyer — but it's a *practical* deal‑killer worth heading off. Two clean options: (a) cap total post‑closing possession at 60 days, or (b) accept the buyer may need to close all‑cash. At minimum, your attorney and the buyer's lender should be told about the rent‑back up front.

**3. Keeping your stay a "license," not a tenancy.**
A post‑closing seller stay — especially one that stretches with an escalating rent‑back — risks being recast as a residential tenancy, which in NJ brings eviction‑process protections (Anti‑Eviction Act) that make removing a holdover slow. § 4.10 frames it as a permissive, time‑limited license with a "no tenancy" recital, and § 4.6 sets a hard Surrender Deadline. That framing helps but isn't bulletproof; the real leverage is the **holdback + holdover penalty**, which is why Note 5 matters.

**4. "Closing delayed beyond ~30 days counts against my no‑cost period" — this one is ambiguous.**
Your sentence can be read two ways. I drafted the **seller‑favorable** version (§ 4.3): only your delay *beyond* 30 days past the scheduled closing eats into your free 30 days. The **buyer‑favorable** alternative is that *any* seller‑caused delay reduces the free period day‑for‑day (so you don't get to both drag the closing and keep all 30 free days). Buyers/their attorney may push for the latter. Also: because Section 43 already gives you a "flexible closing date," we should pin down exactly what the "scheduled Closing date" is that we measure delay against. **Tell me which reading you want and I'll lock the language.**

**5. Size the holdback so it can't run dry while you're still inside.**
Once accrued rent‑back draws the Occupancy Holdback down to zero, the buyer has lost its security — so the holdback should be large enough to cover your worst‑case stay plus a cushion, and the escalating rate should bite hard enough to motivate you to leave on time. That's why § 4.6 sets a hard Surrender Deadline and § 4.7 adds a holdover penalty. Pick a holdback amount and an outside date that you're confident you'll beat.

**6. CO / occupancy inspection timing, given you're staying.**
Most NJ municipal certificates key off a *change of occupancy*. Since occupancy here changes when **you** leave (not at closing), § 5.3 ties any change‑of‑occupancy inspection/certificate to your surrender, with the buyer responsible (consistent with Section 43). Confirm Middletown's specific resale/CO and smoke‑CO‑fire‑extinguisher certification requirements — the smoke/CO/fire‑extinguisher cert is a state safety requirement for transfer and someone has to actually obtain it.

**7. "As‑is" doesn't erase a fraud/known‑latent‑defect duty (and that's fine).**
In NJ, an as‑is clause does not immunize a seller who fraudulently conceals a known latent defect. § 5.5 has you reaffirm your disclosure "to the best of your knowledge," which is the right posture — it's good‑faith and consistent with the law. No action needed; just don't treat "as‑is" as a license to sit on something you actually know about.

## C. Things you didn't ask about but may want to consider

- **Displacement credit direction (§ 5.8).** I read "count toward my holdback period" as a **credit in your favor** — each day a buyer‑approved inspection forces you out adds a free day (or credits a rent‑back day). A few brief hours = one full day, per your instruction. Confirm that's the direction you meant (it benefits you).
- **Personal property / staging left during the rent‑back** — § 4.8 requires broom‑clean surrender free of your belongings; fine as is.
- **Pets, alterations, showings** during your post‑closing stay — usually addressed in a fuller use‑and‑occupancy agreement. Tell me if you want them in.
- **Walk‑through at surrender** — § 4.8 moves the Section 25 walk‑through to your surrender date. Good for you (buyer can't use a pre‑closing walk‑through to hold up closing over condition).
- **Mortgage line in Section 2.** Since financing is now non‑contingent, § 2 declares the Section 2 sub‑allocations "for reference only." Your attorney may prefer to simply restate price/deposit/balance and strike the mortgage line.

## D. What I'd push back on / watch

- The biggest real‑world risk is **Note 2 (the 60‑day lender cap)** colliding with an open‑ended rent‑back. If you genuinely may need more than ~60 days post‑closing, raise it now or the buyer's loan could unravel late.
- Keep the **escalation steep and the holdback generous** — a soft rent‑back rate plus a thin holdback is how sellers end up overstaying with no consequence.
